cd **Core principle:** You do not learn by reading. You learn by struggling.
One hour of active work with a closed book and a pen equals ten hours of passive reading.

Each practice has two parts:
- **Do this** — the physical act, away from any screen
- **Then in the Knowledge Map** — how it gets recorded permanently

---

## The 5 practices

---

### 1 · Read with a pen

**Do this:**
Open the book. Read one equation. Cover it. Derive it yourself on blank paper from the line above. If you cannot — stop. Figure it out. Do not turn the page until you can reproduce it.

After every section: close the book. On blank paper, reproduce the main derivation and the key result from memory. If you cannot write it, you did not understand it. Re-read. Try again.

Draw your own diagram for every physical setup described — before reading the book's version.

**Pace:** 2–3 hours per 10 pages of a graduate textbook. That is correct.

**Then in the Knowledge Map:**
Create a new concept note in the right course folder, using the Concept Note Template. Fill in:
- Core Idea — one sentence, no equations
- Key Equation — the one you just derived on paper
- Derivation Outline — the logical skeleton, 3 steps

The Physical Intuition section stays empty for now — that gets filled after Practice 3.

---
### 2 · Struggle with problems

**Do this:**
Pick 2–3 problems per chapter. Sit with each one for at least 20 minutes before touching any resource. Write down what you know, what you need, and where you are stuck. Keep going.

Pick problems that feel slightly beyond what you think you can do. Never pick the easy ones — solving easy problems only confirms what you already know.

After solving: write one sentence — what physical principle was this problem really about?

**Then in the Knowledge Map:**
Add that sentence to the concept note, under a short "Problem insight" line.

---

### 3 · Explain it without equations

**Do this:**
Close the book. Explain what you just learned out loud in plain words. No equations first — just words. When you get stuck, you have found the exact gap. Go back, fill it, explain again.

The test: can you explain it to someone who has never seen physics, in under one minute, with zero notation? If yes — you own it. If you stumble — you do not.

**Then in the Knowledge Map:**
Write the spoken explanation into the concept note's Physical Intuition section. Write it after speaking it — never before. The constraint of no equations is what forces real understanding into the note.

The concept note is now complete.

---

### 4 · Space your reviews

**Do this:**
Review at increasing intervals — just before you forget.

| After learning | Review action |
|---|---|
| 1 day | Reproduce derivation from memory on blank paper |
| 1 week | Reproduce + explain without equations |
| 1 month | Reproduce + connect to one new thing learned since |
| 3 months | Reproduce — if clean, this concept is permanent |

Can reproduce → push the next review further out.
Cannot reproduce → re-study that section, restart the interval from 1 day.

**Then in the Knowledge Map — every Sunday, 20 minutes:**

Look back through the notes you created or edited this week. Write down, wherever feels natural — a fresh note if you like, or straight onto blank paper:
1. What you learned — 3 sentences
2. New concept notes added — list with links
3. New isomorphisms from the log
4. 3 concepts to review today — not touched in 1+ week
5. A self-test question for each new concept

Then close Obsidian. Blank paper. Answer the self-test questions from memory.

---

### 5 · Log every isomorphism, instantly

**Do this:**
The moment you notice that two things from different topics share the same mathematical structure — write it down. One line. Immediately. Before you continue reading.

Do not trust yourself to remember it later. The moment of recognition is fragile — it disappears within minutes.

**Examples:**

| System A | System B | Shared structure |
|---|---|---|
| Damped oscillator | Ring resonator with loss | Same ODE — Γ/2 is damping |
| Coupled oscillators | CW-CCW backscattering | Same 2×2 eigenvalue matrix |
| Perturbation theory | CMT in photonics | Same mathematical structure |

| Fabry-Pérot resonance | Ring resonator resonance | Same round-trip phase condition |

**Then in the Knowledge Map — capture it before continuing:**

Open the Isomorphism Log directly (or use the QuickAdd shortcut if configured) and add an entry with System A, System B, the shared structure, and why it matters. Add a backlink in both concept notes pointing to each other.

    ↓

    ↓ core idea + key equation + derivation outline

    ↓ 20 min minimum, pick hard ones

    ↓ concept note is now complete

## Monthly gap check — once a month, 15 minutes

At the start of each month, browse the vault deliberately:

1. Which concept notes have no incoming links from other notes? These are isolated — find a connection or flag them for review.
2. Which topics have you read about but never written a concept note for?
3. Which Open Questions are older than 3 weeks with no progress?
4. Which notes are your most connected — your strongest concepts?
5. Which are your least connected — your weakest?

The weakest notes are your reading targets for the next month.

---

## When research blocks you

Something in a paper or simulation does not make sense → that is your reading target.

1. Find the specific chapter in the Physics Roadmap that covers it
2. Read just that section — with a pen
3. Do two problems
4. Create the concept note
5. Return to the research

Physics study is not separate from research. It is what unblocks research.

---

## The one rule above all

**Close the book more than you open it.**

The book is raw material.
The pen is the tool.
The blank paper is where the learning happens.
The Knowledge Map is where it becomes permanent.

But only after the struggle — never instead of it.
