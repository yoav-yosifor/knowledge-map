## Core Idea

Squeezed states satisfy the same minimum-uncertainty bound as [coherent states](Coherent%20States.md), but they don't split the noise evenly between the two [quadratures](Quadrature%20Operators.md). Instead, noise is pushed below the [vacuum](Vacuum%20Fluctuations.md) level in one quadrature, at the cost of extra noise piling up in the other. This redistribution can only be produced by a non-linear medium — no simple linear optical element (a mirror, a lens, an ordinary beamsplitter) can create it.

## Key Equation

$$
\hat S(r) = \exp\left[\frac12\left(r^*\hat a^2-r\hat a^{\dagger2}\right)\right], \qquad \hat S^\dagger(r)\,\hat a\,\hat S(r) = \cosh r\,\hat a+\sinh r\,\hat a^\dagger
$$

**Derived From:** the interaction Hamiltonian of a medium responding quadratically to the applied field, $\hat H_{int}=i(r^*\hat a^2-r\hat a^{\dagger2})$, whose generated unitary is expanded via the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff conjugation series using $[\hat a^2,\hat a^\dagger]=2\hat a$ — full proof in [Squeezing Operator Derivation](Squeezing%20Operator%20Derivation.md).

## Supporting Formulas

- $\hat S^\dagger\hat X\hat S=e^r\hat X$, $\hat S^\dagger\hat P\hat S=e^{-r}\hat P$ — one quadrature is stretched, the conjugate one compressed
- $\Delta X=\dfrac{e^r}{\sqrt2}$, $\Delta P=\dfrac{e^{-r}}{\sqrt2}$, $\Delta X\Delta P=\tfrac12$ — still a minimum-uncertainty state, just an unequally-shared one
- At a general measurement angle $\theta$ relative to the squeezing axis $\phi$: $\Delta X_\theta^2=\tfrac12\bigl(e^{2r}\cos^2(\phi-\theta)+e^{-2r}\sin^2(\phi-\theta)\bigr)$
- Squeezed vacuum $|0_r\rangle=\hat S(r)|0\rangle$ carries real power despite its name: $\langle\hat n\rangle=\sinh^2r\neq0$
- $|\text{SMSV}\rangle$ is built entirely from photon pairs — a direct signature of the $\hat a^2,\hat a^{\dagger2}$ structure of the generating Hamiltonian: $|\text{SMSV}\rangle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}\sum_n(-e^{i\phi}\tanh r)^n\dfrac{\sqrt{(2n)!}}{2^nn!}|2n\rangle$

## Derivation Outline

1. Posit a quadratic (two-photon) interaction Hamiltonian, generated by a non-linear medium, and its associated unitary squeezing operator $\hat S(r)$.
2. Expand $\hat S^\dagger\hat a\hat S$ via the BCH conjugation series using $[\hat a^2,\hat a^\dagger]=2\hat a$, resumming the series into hyperbolic functions of $r$.
3. Combine the transformed $\hat a,\hat a^\dagger$ into the $\hat X,\hat P$ transformation, revealing the stretch/compress structure directly.
4. Apply $\hat S(r)$ to the vacuum to get the squeezed vacuum and its photon-number statistics.

## Physical Intuition

A coherent state spreads its unavoidable quantum noise evenly between the two quadratures — it's "classical" in the sense that it doesn't play favorites. A squeezed state makes a deliberate trade: it borrows some of the noise out of one quadrature and dumps it into the other, so that one direction in phase space becomes quieter than the vacuum itself while the perpendicular direction becomes correspondingly noisier — the total uncertainty product stays at the same minimum value, just rearranged. This can't happen through any simple linear process like the ones that generate coherent states (a classical current pushing on the field); it requires a genuinely non-linear medium that couples photons in pairs, which is also why squeezed vacuum, despite containing "zero photons" in name, actually carries measurable power — it's built entirely out of photon pairs created together by the same non-linear process, never a single stray photon.
