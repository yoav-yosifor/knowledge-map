## Core Idea

The modulus (absolute value) of a complex number is its distance from the origin in the complex plane, generalizing the familiar absolute value of the reals.

## Key Equation

$$
|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2} \qquad (z=a+bi)
$$

**Derived From:** in the vector representation $(a,b)\leftrightarrow a+bi$, the distance from the origin by Pythagoras.

## Supporting Formulas

- $$ |z|^2=z\cdot\bar z $$ — links the modulus to the conjugate
- $$ |z|\in\mathbb{R} $$ —
- $$ |z|\ge 0,\quad |z|=0 \iff z=0 $$ —
- $$ |z_1z_2|=|z_1|\cdot|z_2| $$ — the modulus is multiplicative
- $$ |z_1+z_2|\le|z_1|+|z_2| $$ — triangle inequality
- $$ |z|=|-z|=|\bar z| $$ —
