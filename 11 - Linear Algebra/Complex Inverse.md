## Core Idea

The multiplicative inverse of a nonzero complex number — the $w$ with $zw=1$ — is found by multiplying numerator and denominator of $1/z$ by the conjugate, which makes the denominator real.

## Key Equation

$$
z^{-1}=\frac{1}{z}=\frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{\bar z}{\bar z}=\frac{a-bi}{|z|^2}=\frac{a}{|z|^2}-\frac{b}{|z|^2}\,i
$$

**Derived From:** multiplying by $\bar z/\bar z$ and using $z\bar z=|z|^2\in\mathbb{R}$.

## Supporting Formulas

- $$ \frac{1}{1+i}=\frac{1}{1+i}\cdot\frac{1-i}{1-i}=\frac{1-i}{2}=\frac12-\frac12 i $$ — worked example

## Derivation Outline

1. Write $z^{-1}=1/z$ and multiply by $\bar z/\bar z$.
2. The denominator becomes $z\bar z=|z|^2$, a real number.
3. Split into real and imaginary parts: $\frac{a}{|z|^2}-\frac{b}{|z|^2}i$.
