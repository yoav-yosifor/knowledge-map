## Core Idea

The EPR argument uses the perfect correlations of a maximally entangled state to claim that quantum mechanics must be incomplete. If Alice can predict Bob's outcome with certainty without ever disturbing his system, the argument goes, that outcome must have been predetermined all along. Yet quantum mechanics assigns it no definite value before the measurement — which EPR took as evidence the theory is missing something.

## Key Equation

$$
|\psi^-\rangle = \frac{|01\rangle - |10\rangle}{\sqrt2} \qquad \text{(the EPR/singlet state)}
$$

**Derived From:** the singlet is the maximally anti-correlated [Bell state](Bell%20States.md) — measuring Alice and Bob's spins along the same axis always gives opposite outcomes, for any choice of axis.

## Supporting Formulas

- No faster-than-light signaling: Bob's local statistics are always $\rho_B = I/2$, independent of Alice's measurement choice or basis — the correlation is only observable after classical communication

## Derivation Outline

1. Alice can predict Bob's outcome with certainty (perfect correlation, any shared measurement axis)
2. By locality, Alice's measurement cannot disturb Bob's system
3. Therefore Bob's outcome must have been predetermined (an element of reality)
4. Quantum mechanics assigns no definite value to that observable beforehand — so it must be incomplete (EPR's conclusion)

## Physical Intuition

Quantum mechanics is nonlocal in correlations but local in signaling: if Alice and Bob measure an entangled state in the same basis their outcomes are perfectly correlated, but Bob's result alone is random and reveals nothing about Alice's result or even whether she measured at all. The correlation only becomes visible after they compare notes classically — so entanglement cannot be used to signal faster than light.
