## Core Idea

[Superposition](Superposition.md) lets a quantum computer evolve multiple computational paths at once. But quantum advantage doesn't come from this parallelism alone: measurement collapses the state down to a single outcome, so simply running many paths in parallel buys you nothing by itself. The advantage only appears once [Interference](Interference.md) is used to control which paths reinforce each other and which cancel.

## Derivation Outline

1. [Superposition](Superposition.md) lets a quantum computer's unitary evolve many computational paths at once.
2. Measurement collapses that superposition down to a single, randomly selected outcome.
3. So merely "computing on all inputs at once" yields nothing extra by itself.
4. Quantum advantage requires [Interference](Interference.md) to reinforce the paths carrying the desired answer and cancel the rest.

## Physical Intuition

Quantum advantage arises not from parallelism alone, but from interference control — measurement collapses the superposition to one outcome, so simply "computing on all inputs at once" gives you nothing until the amplitudes are made to interfere.
