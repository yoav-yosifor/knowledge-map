## Core Idea

Quantum systems are neither waves nor particles — they are state vectors whose behavior depends on context. Unitary evolution looks wave-like, producing interference; [measurement](Quantum%20Measurement.md) looks particle-like, producing localized outcomes. The apparent duality disappears once state evolution and information extraction are treated as separate stages.

## Derivation Outline

1. Separate the two stages of a quantum system's life: unitary evolution (unmeasured) and [measurement](Quantum%20Measurement.md).
2. During unitary evolution, amplitudes add and interfere — wave-like fringes (e.g. double-slit).
3. During measurement, the state collapses to one localized outcome — particle-like.
4. These are two different operations on the same state vector, not two contradictory natures — the "duality" dissolves once evolution and measurement are treated as separate stages.

## Physical Intuition

The old puzzle of whether light or matter is "really" a wave or "really" a particle turns out to be a confusion about which stage of the process you're looking at, not a genuine contradiction in nature. While a quantum system is left alone to evolve — traveling through a double slit, say, without anyone checking which path it took — its behavior is governed by rules that let different possibilities add and subtract from each other, building up bright fringes where possibilities reinforce and dark gaps where they cancel, which is exactly the signature of a wave. But the moment you extract information from the system by measuring it, you no longer see spread-out interference — you see one specific, localized outcome, exactly the kind of thing a particle would give you, precisely because measurement forces the system to commit to a single result out of everything it could have been. There's no real contradiction here, because these are two completely different operations performed on the same underlying object: the state vector itself is neither a wave nor a particle, it simply behaves one way while it's being left to evolve on its own and a different way the instant you interrogate it for a definite answer. Once quiet evolution and active measurement are kept clearly separated in your mind, the supposed duality isn't a paradox to be resolved — it's just two different questions being asked of the same thing at two different times.
