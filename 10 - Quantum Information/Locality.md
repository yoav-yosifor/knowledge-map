## Core Idea

Locality is the principle that physical influences cannot propagate faster than light. Concretely, this means actions performed in one location cannot instantaneously affect outcomes in a distant, spacelike-separated region. It is rooted in special relativity, and Einstein treated it as non-negotiable.

## Derivation Outline

1. Take two events at spacelike separation — not enough time for a light signal to connect them.
2. Special relativity forbids any physical influence from outpacing light between such events.
3. So an action at one location cannot affect the outcome at the other before light could arrive.
4. Any theory that seems to require instantaneous influence across such separation is therefore judged incomplete or wrong (Einstein's stance).

## Physical Intuition

Locality is really just the demand that cause and effect respect the speed limit built into relativity: whatever you do to one system, the consequences of that action can only reach another system by physically traveling there, and nothing — no signal, no influence, no information — can make that trip faster than light. If two events happen at locations far enough apart, and close enough together in time, that not even a light signal could get from one to the other before the second event occurs, then nothing you do at the first location is allowed to have any effect whatsoever on the outcome at the second. For Einstein this wasn't a minor technical assumption to be argued away — it was the bedrock that made relativity a coherent theory of cause and effect at all, and he treated any theory that seemed to require instantaneous influences across space as either incomplete or simply wrong, because such influences would let you build a notion of simultaneity and causal order that clashes with everything relativity says about how different observers can disagree about which of two distant events happened first. That's precisely why quantum entanglement, when Einstein first confronted it, looked to him like it must be pointing to something missing from quantum mechanics rather than to a genuine violation of locality — because giving up locality meant giving up the entire relativistic picture of the universe he refused to abandon.
